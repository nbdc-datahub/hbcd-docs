## Instrument Details

The **Deferred Imitation Task** measures long-term memory through deferred imitation of two event sequences using novel props:

- **Gong:** 3-steps sequence with three target actions performed in a specific order.
- **Beary-Go-Round:** 4-steps sequence with four target actions performed in a randomized order.

After a minimum delay 10 minutes, children are assessed on their recall of the actions and their order. Administration, prop presentation, and supporting prompts are standardized.

### Administration Details
A brief 2-3 minute warm-up with slinkys introduces the child to the imitation task. RAs rate on a 1-7 scale how much the child "warmed up" to the task and "imitated" actions during the warm up activity. Baseline data is acquired for each sequence to control for problem solving/creativity.

The assessment proceeds as follows:

1. Warm-up.
1. Event 1: Baseline followed by modeling (twice)
1. Event 2: Baseline followed by modeling (twice)
1. Delay of at least 10 minutes (length of delay recorded to control for variations in duration)
5. Recall for event 1, followed by recall for event 2.

A target action is scored only the first time the child demonstrates it, during BASELINE and then again during RECALL, for each event sequence (e.g., "ring the bell"). The child's ability to recall is scored as the number of target actions completed ("target actions" in the correct temporal order, termed "pairs of actions") during RECALL. Pairs of actions that occur in the correct temporal order are automatically calculated (e.g., "hang the bell" followed by "ring the bell" = 1 pair). Actions and pairs of actions are summed across the two event sequences at baseline and recall. RAs record errors when applicable.

    An SOP was provided with detailed administration and scoring instructions. Training was conducted via Zoom and in-person sessions and consisted of detailed descriptions and example videos.
    Each RA went through a two-step certification process – administration and scoring. For the first step, each RA administered the V06 deferred imitation protocol to an adult, video recorded it, filled out a self-assessment form (with scores), and submitted these materials to the Infant Testing Core for review.
    An expert (TR, ZC, IS, SD, NS, AG) reviewed the video for both administration and scoring accuracy. Feedback was always given. If the _administration_ met a standard threshold (no major administration errors), the RA was certified to administer the task.
    If the _administration_ did not meet threshold, the RA was asked to submit another video. If scoring was inaccurate, this was communicated to the RA so they could correct it in the second certification step. This process continued until the RA submitted a video that met threshold, at which point RAs were certified in administration and released to proceed to the second step.

    ### Scoring Certification
    RAs who were certified to administer the task then video recorded their administration to a child (pilot OR main study) participant. RAs scored the child’s performance (in LORIS for main study participants, on the self-assessment form for pilots) and an expert (TR, ZC, SD, NS) reviewed the video for both administration and scoring accuracy.
    Feedback was always given. If the administration met a standard threshold (i.e., no administration or scoring errors), the RA was certified in scoring the task and fully certified to administer deferred imitation in the main study without the need for expert video review.
    If the administration did not meet threshold, the RA was asked to submit another video with a different child participant and submit the video for review.

    **Score corrections:** If during any video review, scores were incorrect, the RA was asked to correct them in LORIS _and make a note to this effect in the RA comments_. The expert reviewer ensured the scores were updated accurately and noted this in the certification log notes.
    After the two-step certification process, RAs were released to administer and score deferred imitation to child participants without video review.
